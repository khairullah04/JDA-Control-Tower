**Top 3 risks**

1. Integrity is not enforced where the data lives. Report documents are writable by the whole internet (#1), any self-registered account can change budgets and spend the AI key (#2), and the passkeys guarding the rest are short, committed and brute-forceable (#3). The finance collections are the one properly closed area.
2. Silent data loss in ordinary use. Stale whole-array saves and unhandled write failures in the portal (#4), the task-relinking bug (#5), one-click sample and clear buttons (#6), doubled sanctioned amounts on re-import (#8), and the file-store fallback (#9) all lose or corrupt data without an error, and only the finance register has an audit trail to recover from.
3. Operability for one maintainer. The largest and most-changed code (`report.js`, `editor.js`) has no tests and two parallel editors (#17, #30), server error paths are thin (#13), the read-everything-on-every-click pattern will slow as invoices accumulate (#11), and there is no deployment or backup runbook (#19).

**What's done well**

- The money model is right: whole minor units, BigInt for every multiply or divide, one rounding rule, each record keeping its own rate and tax rate, all in one module shared by browser and server and pinned by hand-worked tests (`jda/web/js/finance/money.js`, `core.js`, `jda/server/finance-core.test.mjs`).
- The finance register is designed for trust: server-side validation, an audit line committed with every change, soft delete and restore, a dry-run preview before any import, and rules that close the `finance*` collections to browsers (`service.js`, `routes/finance.js`, `firestore.rules:8-10`).
- Untrusted input is handled carefully and consistently: output is escaped throughout both sites, stored rich text passes an allow-list sanitiser on save and on display (`portal/js/blocks.js:47-81`), and AI output is clamped and enum-checked with unreadable files reported as "skipped" rather than guessed (`jda/server/portal-sync.js:37-64`).

**Score:** 5/10. The CJI3 and Payment Table core is production-grade and well tested, but the portal's world-writable storage, open sign-up, weak committed passkeys and several silent data-loss paths mean the system as a whole is not ready to hold data people rely on.
