**Top 3 risks**

1. **"Actual (paid)" is a manually set flag with no payment evidence and no period lock (1, 4, 25).** There is no payment date, amount or reference, months restate whenever a status changes, and the Over/Under flag ignores approved invoices at exactly the point a payment decision is made.
2. **Duplicates on both sides of the comparison (2, 3).** One invoice can be counted in full on two projects or under a slightly different number, and sanctioned amounts double on a repeated import, which hides overspend.
3. **Imports can silently omit or misplace money (5, 6, 7, 13, 22, 23).** Rows after a blank line are dropped, credit notes are refused, day-first dates move invoices to the wrong month, and decimal commas or unknown statuses change amounts or status without warning. Nothing reconciles the import to a control total.

**What's done well**

- Money is held as whole sen with one rounding rule (half away from zero) and exact arithmetic throughout. I reworked every hand-calculated test figure (USD 12,345.67 at 4.2150 = 52,037.00 / 4,162.96 / 56,199.96; 813,000.00 to 878,040.00; variance -33,753.56) and they are right.
- Stored figures do not change by themselves: each record keeps its own rate, rate date, source and tax rate; a tax-setting change touches no existing record; Recalculate is deliberate and logged field by field; deletes are soft and restorable.
- The Payment Table is derived on the spot from the same records as the register, so each row figure traces to its invoices, and the server (not the browser) validates, calculates and writes every finance change with its audit lines.

**Score:** 6/10. The arithmetic and the preservation of historical rates are sound, but the controls around the figures (payment evidence, period lock, duplicates, import completeness, attributable audit identity) are not yet strong enough for payment decisions without independent checking.
